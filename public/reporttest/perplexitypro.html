Report Conclusion

The opportunity is real, but the practical route is not a generic paid AI course. The winning path is trusted, local, funded delivery for people who need confidence with phones, scams, health services, forms, job search, and only then AI.

Primary wedge

B2B2C institutional contracts (council/library/housing association digital inclusion programmes), using scam safety, NHS app help, and phone confidence as the access product, with AI introduced only after trust is established.

No-go wedge

Generic consumer AI courses sold directly as AI training. Direct consumer willingness to pay is weak, free alternatives are strong, and AI branding deters many of the most excluded users.

Buyer reality

The end user often has the need, but councils, libraries, NHS programmes, housing associations, employers, charities, banks, telecoms, or adult children are more likely to pay.

Risks & Constraints

The risks are not cosmetic. A credible business in this market needs safeguarding, plain-English consent, data minimisation, DBS-checked delivery where appropriate, and firm boundaries around benefits, financial, health, and legal advice.

Free competition

Government, banks, telecoms and charities already provide free digital skills support. A paid offer must attach to trusted local delivery, outcomes, or institutional capacity gaps.

Low direct willingness to pay

Most excluded users cannot or will not pay directly. The buyer is usually the council, employer, housing association, charity, NHS programme, bank, or adult child.

AI trust gap

AI branding can repel the very audience that most needs help. The offer should lead with phone confidence, scam safety, NHS App help, job help, and life admin.

Safeguarding and data handling

Sessions may involve vulnerable adults, health portals, finances, benefits, and AI tools that process user inputs. Clear boundaries and consent are essential.

Labour intensity

One-to-one help is valuable but scales poorly. Train-the-trainer, employer cohorts, and institutional contracts are needed to grow beyond a small practice.

Recommended Pilot

Six months; one city or town; one institutional partner (housing association or council); one product (Scam Safety + Phone Confidence group workshop, 6–10 sessions of 10 people each); simple pre/post confidence survey for evaluation. Total reach: 60–100 participants. Revenue target: £5,000–£15,000 per contract. Measure: participant confidence scores, institutional partner satisfaction, referral pipeline from first contract to second.

1

Pick one town or city

Do not start national. Choose a local authority or housing association footprint where referrals can be concentrated.

2

Sell one institutional pilot

Target a £5k-£15k six-month pilot: 6-10 group sessions, 60-100 participants, free to the end user.

3

Lead with scam safety and phone confidence

Treat AI as a practical extension after trust is established, not as the headline.

4

Measure before and after confidence

Capture participant confidence, successful task completion, partner satisfaction, and referral demand.

5

Package the curriculum

Only after the pilot, convert the material into a train-the-trainer offer for councils, libraries, housing associations, and charities.
